\title{StableMax-PPO: Finite-Budget RLHF with a Max--Stability Trade-off}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Best-of-\(N\) sampling is routinely used when a language model is given a finite response budget and the best candidate is selected. We formulate this setting as terminal-reward RLHF with a frozen ordinal Moment RM trained from HelpSteer2 repeated helpfulness ratings and preference pairs; its predictive mean is a nominal rating signal and its conditional variance is a model-based signal of rating disagreement under that annotation protocol. We derive an exact finite-support Best-of-\(N\) objective and a one-sided calibration correction for upper-tail optimization. \method{} uses the resulting Rao--Blackwellized marginal credit inside PPO, together with hard KL rollback, prompt-only baselines, lagged scaling, and non-inferiority floors on nominal reward and an independent Quality RM. Across matched baselines, the method occupies a stability-favoring Pareto point: it preserves competitive finite-budget maximum performance while reducing policy drift and maintaining quality safeguards. The intended contribution is a principled and tunable max--stability trade-off, not universal dominance on either axis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Large language models are increasingly deployed with a finite generation budget:
the system samples several responses and returns, reranks, or inspects the best
candidate. Training such a policy only for the expected score of one sample
optimizes a different functional from the one used at inference. The gap is
especially visible when the evaluator is ordinal and the deployment objective
is an upper-tail statistic. A policy can improve its average score while
leaving the probability of a very high-scoring candidate unchanged, or it can
increase that probability by concentrating mass in a small and brittle region
of its output distribution.

Directly optimizing a finite-budget maximum has a second difficulty. Reward
models are imperfect, and errors in the upper tail are amplified by taking a
maximum over many candidates. In our setting, the frozen Moment reward model
is trained from the repeated helpfulness ratings and preference pairs in
HelpSteer2 \citep{wang2024helpsteer2}. Its five-category predictive mean is a
model-implied expected rating; its conditional variance is a model-based
indicator of disagreement in that repeated-annotation protocol. We use this
distributional information to compute the maximum exactly, but do not treat
variance as a free reward. The policy is instead constrained by a one-sided
calibration envelope, a nominal-mean floor, an independent Quality RM floor,
and a reference-policy KL budget. These safeguards target different failure
modes: upper-tail misspecification, degradation of typical responses,
over-optimization of the training evaluator, and uncontrolled policy drift.

We introduce \method, a PPO realization of this constrained objective. The
method has two separate components. First, a finite-support order-statistic
identity converts frozen ordinal probabilities into an exact robust maximum
and an exact leave-one-out credit for each sampled response. Second, a
conservative update controller combines that credit with mean and quality dual
variables, a KL penalty, and an exact rollback of the policy and optimizer
state when a tentative update exceeds the hard KL level. The resulting design
is not intended to dominate max-oriented methods on every endpoint. It exposes
a useful operating point: maximum performance is traded against policy
stability and evaluator safeguards, so a practitioner can make the trade-off
explicit instead of hiding it in an unreported tuning choice.

In the frozen three-seed evaluation, this operating point has lower held-out
KL than the mean- and group-relative baselines while retaining a competitive
robust maximum. The strongest max-oriented baselines remain higher on the
maximum endpoint, and the preregistered all-gates criterion is therefore
reported as failed rather than redefined.

Our contributions are fourfold. (1) We give an exact finite-\(N\) objective for
a bounded ordinal reward model and a closed-form one-sided robust correction.
(2) We derive an exact Rao--Blackwellized marginal policy credit and show its
relation to the constrained PPO surrogate. (3) We provide a compact PPO
algorithm with prompt-only baselines, quality duals, full-categorical KL
measurement, and batch-level hard rollback. (4) We evaluate the method under
a frozen, multi-seed protocol and report both favorable and failed gates. The
aggregate results support a max--stability trade-off, not universal
superiority or direct human-preference improvement.

\section{Problem Setup}
\label{sec:problem-setup}

This paper studies terminal-reward language-model reinforcement learning with a fixed best-of-\(N\) response budget.  The objective is the exact upper-tail value induced by a frozen calibrated ordinal reward model, together with predeclared robustness, mean-quality, independent-quality, and KL safeguards.  The method does not rely on a Gaussian reward assumption and does not claim direct human-preference superiority before the confirmatory evaluation is complete.  A prompt \(x\sim\mathcal D\) and a
response \(Y=(a_1,\ldots,a_H)\) induce states
\(s_h=(x,a_{<h})\) and policy probability

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sequence-policy}
\pi_\theta(Y\mid x)
=\prod_{h=1}^{H}
\pi_\theta(a_h\mid s_h).
\end{equation}

The response receives one terminal evaluation rather than an artificial sum
of token-level rewards.  This is the standard response-level RLHF setting
\citep{christiano2017deep,stiennon2020learning,ouyang2022training}.  Our
target is not the expected rating of one response.  It is the expected best
rating attainable from a fixed sampling budget, while preserving nominal
quality and limiting policy drift.

\subsection{Problem formulation and notation}
\label{subsec:problem-notation}

For each prompt, the policy generates a group of \(N\) responses

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:iid-policy-group}
Y_{1:N}=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_N),
\qquad
Y_j\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}
\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x).
\end{equation}

The integer \(N\) is the response budget at which the policy is trained and
evaluated.  Conditional on \((x,Y_j)\), let \(R_j\) denote a stochastic
human-rating outcome.  We make the following assumptions explicit.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A1)] Conditional on \((x,Y_{1:N})\), the rating variables
\(R_1,\ldots,R_N\) are independent draws from their respective
annotation distributions.
\item[(A2)] The reward model, its calibration parameters, the reference
policy, and the independent Quality RM are frozen during policy optimization.
\item[(A3)] \(\pi_\theta(Y\mid x)\) is differentiable in
\(\theta\), its support does not change locally, and differentiation may be
interchanged with expectation.
\end{enumerate}

Assumption (A1) concerns repeated annotation outcomes conditional on generated
responses; it does not assert that tokens inside a response are independent.
Assumption (A2) is essential because the robust objective below is defined
relative to a fixed predictive distribution.  Assumption (A3) is the standard
regularity condition for likelihood-ratio policy gradients
\citep{williams1992simple,sutton1999policy}.

Throughout, \(p_{\omega,r}(x,y)\) is the frozen probability of rating
\(r\), \(F_{\omega}(t\mid x,y)\) is its CDF,
\(\mu_\omega(x,y)\) and
\(\sigma_\omega^2(x,y)\) are its observable mean and variance,
\(Q_\eta(x,y)\) is an independently trained scalar quality score,
and \(\pi_\mathrm{ref}\) is a frozen reference policy.  The subscripts
\(\omega\) and \(\eta\) distinguish the two evaluators; neither
belongs to the trainable policy parameter \(\theta\).

\subsection{Relationship to prior objectives}
\label{subsec:scope}

\method{} is a policy-gradient method implemented through PPO; it is not a new
optimizer or a new reinforcement-learning paradigm.  Inference-aware training,
pass@\(k\), max@\(k\), and Best-of-\(N\)-aware policy gradients have
already been developed for scalar or verifiable rewards
\citep{chow2025inference,tang2025inference,walder2025pkpo,
bagirov2025best,li2026posttraining,poiani2026maxk}.  We therefore do not claim
the first max@\(k\) objective.

The distinction is the object being maximized. Here the reward model predicts
a calibrated, bounded ordinal annotation distribution; the finite-\(N\)
order statistic is computed exactly; calibration uncertainty is propagated
through a closed-form one-sided ambiguity set; and the upper-tail objective is
optimized subject to nominal-mean, independent-quality, and KL safeguards.
We call this combination \textbf{\method}.

This construction is also distinct from distributional RL.  Distributional RL
learns a distribution of discounted returns through a distributional Bellman
operator \citep{bellemare2017distributional,dabney2018distributional}.
\method{} learns no return distribution and performs no distributional Bellman
backup.  A frozen model predicts one terminal annotation distribution, and the
policy applies a finite-sample order statistic to iid responses.  The
ambiguity-set component is related to robust MDPs and distributionally robust
optimization \citep{iyengar2005robust,nilim2005robust}, but it is defined
over five terminal rating categories rather than transition kernels.

\subsection{Ordinal reward distribution}
\label{subsec:ordinal-rm}

Let the observable rating space be
\(\mathcal R=\{0,1,2,3,4\}\).  For a completed
prompt--response pair, the frozen reward model predicts

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ordinal-probabilities}
p_{\omega,r}(x,y)
:=\Pr_\omega\{R(x,y)=r\mid x,y\},
\qquad
r\in\mathcal R,
\qquad
\sum_{r=0}^{4}p_{\omega,r}(x,y)=1.
\end{equation}

The model uses an ordinal-probit observation head.  It emits a latent location
\(m_\omega(x,y)\) and a positive latent scale
\(s_\omega(x,y)\).  Given frozen cutpoints
\(c_1<\cdots<c_4\), the category probabilities are

\begin{align}
p_{\omega,0}
&=\Phi\!\left(\frac{c_1-m_\omega}{s_\omega}\right),\\
p_{\omega,r}
&=\Phi\!\left(\frac{c_{r+1}-m_\omega}{s_\omega}\right)
-\Phi\!\left(\frac{c_r-m_\omega}{s_\omega}\right),
\quad r=1,2,3,\\
p_{\omega,4}
&=1-\Phi\!\left(\frac{c_4-m_\omega}{s_\omega}\right).
\end{align}

This is an ordinal observation model, not an unbounded Gaussian reward
\citep{mccullagh1980regression}.  Its observable moments are induced by
the same five probabilities:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ordinal-moments}
\mu_\omega(x,y)
=\sum_{r=0}^{4}r p_{\omega,r}(x,y),
\qquad
\sigma_\omega^2(x,y)
=\sum_{r=0}^{4}
(r-\mu_\omega(x,y))^2p_{\omega,r}(x,y).
\end{equation}

Repeated-rating likelihood and disagreement supervise the conditional scale.
Accordingly, \(\sigma_\omega^2\) represents conditional variation of
ratings under the stated annotation protocol.  It is not epistemic
uncertainty, distance from a preference boundary, or a stand-alone bonus
\citep{kendall2017uncertainties,lou2024uncertainty,sun2025purm}.
Proper scoring and calibration are required because the full distribution,
not only its mean, enters the policy objective
\citep{gneiting2007strictly,guo2017calibration}.

All objectives below are conditional on this frozen reward model.
Consequently, \(R\) denotes a model-implied annotation draw, not an
observed label on a newly generated response and not an objective measure of
human utility.

\subsection{Exact finite-\texorpdfstring{\(N\)}{N} upper-tail objective}
\label{subsec:ordinal-objective}

For candidate \(j\), define

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:candidate-cdf}
F_{\omega,j}(t)
:=\Pr_\omega\{R_j\le t\mid x,Y_j\}
=\sum_{r=0}^{t}p_{\omega,r}(x,Y_j),
\qquad t=0,\ldots,4.
\end{equation}

The nominal conditional Best-of-\(N\) value and its population objective
are

\begin{align}
h_N^{\mathrm{nom}}(x,Y_{1:N})
&:=\mathbb E_\omega\!\left[
\max_{1\le j\le N}R_j
\middle|x,Y_{1:N}\right],\\
J_N^{\mathrm{nom}}(\theta)
&:=\mathbb E_{x\sim\mathcal D,\;
Y_{1:N}\sim\pi_\theta}
\left[h_N^{\mathrm{nom}}(x,Y_{1:N})\right].
\label{eq:nominal-objective}
\end{align}

Finite support and conditional independence yield

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ordinal-max-closed}
\boxed{
h_N^{\mathrm{nom}}(x,Y_{1:N})
=\sum_{t=1}^{4}
\left[
1-\prod_{j=1}^{N}F_{\omega,j}(t-1)
\right].
}
\end{equation}

Equation \cref{eq:ordinal-max-closed} is exact.  It introduces neither
sampled rating noise nor a Gaussian approximation.  The upper endpoint four
is part of the target annotation protocol.

For interpretability, we additionally define the top-category component

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:any-four}
U_N^{\mathrm{nom}}(\theta)
:=\mathbb E_{x,Y_{1:N}}
\left[
1-\prod_{j=1}^{N}
\bigl(1-p_{\omega,4}(x,Y_j)\bigr)
\right].
\end{equation}

This is exactly the probability, under the frozen ordinal RM, that at least
one of the \(N\) candidates receives rating four.  It is not equal to the
Gaussian tail quantity \(1-\Phi(\kappa_N)\).  Because it retains only
the highest threshold, \(U_N^{\mathrm{nom}}\) is a diagnostic and an
ablation objective rather than the primary estimand.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

The design goal is explicit: maximize the finite-budget upper tail while
keeping the policy close to the reference and preserving ordinary and
independent quality. Thus the method is a controlled operating point on a
max--stability frontier. The calibration correction is one component of this
control; it does not turn rating variance into a bonus.

\subsection{Robust finite-budget objective}
\label{subsec:robust-objective}

Finite-\(N\) maxima can amplify small upper-tail calibration errors.  We
therefore construct a one-sided ambiguity radius using a validation split that
is frozen before policy optimization.  Let
\(g=1,\ldots,G_{\mathrm{cal}}\) index independent prompt clusters,
and let \(\mathcal A_g\) contain the annotated response events for
prompt \(g\).  For threshold \(t\in\{0,1,2,3\}\), define

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cluster-residual}
D_{g,t}
=\frac{1}{|\mathcal A_g|}
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_g}
\left[
\mathbf 1\{R_{g,a}\le t\}
-F_{\omega,g,a}(t)
\right].
\end{equation}

Responses and repeated labels within one prompt are aggregated before
concentration.  Let

\begin{align}
\widehat d_t
&=\frac{1}{G_{\mathrm{cal}}}
\sum_{g=1}^{G_{\mathrm{cal}}}D_{g,t},\\
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}
&=\min\!\left\{1,
\max_{t=0,\ldots,3}(\widehat d_t)_+
+\sqrt{
\frac{\log(4/\alpha_{\mathrm{cal}})}
{2G_{\mathrm{cal}}}}
\right\}.
\label{eq:epsilon-calibration}
\end{align}

Here \(\alpha_{\mathrm{cal}}\) is the predeclared one-sided
calibration error probability.  For any nominal probability vector
\(p\in\Delta^4\), where \(\Delta^4\) is the simplex over
five ratings, define

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ambiguity-set}
\mathcal U_\varepsilon(p)
:=\left\{
q\in\Delta^4:
F_q(t)\le
\min\{1,F_p(t)+\varepsilon\},
\quad t=0,1,2,3
\right\}.
\end{equation}

This set permits more probability mass on low ratings in the harmful
direction for maximum optimization.  It does not reward variance by itself.
For candidate \(j\), define

\begin{align}
\overline F_j(t)
&:=\min\{1,F_{\omega,j}(t)
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}\},
\quad t=0,1,2,3,\\
\overline F_j(-1)&:=0,
\qquad
\overline F_j(4):=1,\\
\overline p_{j,r}
&:=\overline F_j(r)-\overline F_j(r-1),
\quad r=0,\ldots,4.
\label{eq:robust-probabilities}
\end{align}

The robust conditional value is the worst finite-\(N\) maximum over the
product ambiguity set:

\begin{align}
h_N^{\mathrm{rob}}(x,Y_{1:N})
&:=\inf_{
q_{1:N}\in
\prod_{j=1}^{N}
\mathcal U_{
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}}
(p_{\omega,j})}
\mathbb E_{
R_{1:N}\sim\bigotimes_{j=1}^{N}q_j}
\left[\max_{1\le j\le N}R_j\right]\\
&=\sum_{t=1}^{4}
\left[
1-\prod_{j=1}^{N}\overline F_j(t-1)
\right].
\label{eq:robust-max-closed}
\end{align}

The primary policy objective is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:robust-primary}
J_N^{\mathrm{rob}}(\theta)
:=\mathbb E_{x\sim\mathcal D,\;
Y_{1:N}\sim\pi_\theta}
\left[h_N^{\mathrm{rob}}(x,Y_{1:N})\right].
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact marginal credit}
\label{subsec:robust-credit}

Let \(\overline R_i\sim\overline p_i\) and
\(\overline M_{-i}
=\max_{j\ne i}\overline R_j\).  We define the marginal
contribution of response \(i\) as follows, with the empty maximum defined as
zero when \(N=1\):

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:robust-credit}
q_i^{\mathrm{rob}}(x,Y_{1:N})
:=\mathbb E\left[
(\overline R_i-\overline M_{-i})_+
\middle|x,Y_{1:N}
\right].
\end{equation}

Its exact form is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:robust-credit-closed}
\boxed{
q_i^{\mathrm{rob}}
=\sum_{t=1}^{4}
\left[1-\overline F_i(t-1)\right]
\prod_{j\ne i}\overline F_j(t-1).
}
\end{equation}

This credit integrates out the stochastic rating outcome while retaining
prompt and response sampling.  It is therefore a Rao--Blackwellized policy
credit, not a deterministic statement about human ratings
\citep{casella1996rao}.

\subsection{Safeguards and constrained objective}
\label{subsec:constrained-objective}

Optimizing a maximum can concentrate quality in a small fraction of the
response distribution while degrading typical samples.  It can also exploit
errors specific to the ordinal RM.  We therefore define two expected-score
safeguards:

\begin{align}
M(\theta)
&:=\mathbb E_{x,Y\sim\pi_\theta}
[\mu_\omega(x,Y)],\\
Q(\theta)
&:=\mathbb E_{x,Y\sim\pi_\theta}
[Q_\eta(x,Y)].
\end{align}

The constants \(m_0\) and \(q_0\) are predeclared
non-inferiority floors derived from the frozen reference policy.  Their
estimation is an experimental-design choice and is specified in
\cref{subsec:experimental-protocol}, not built into the mathematical
definition of \method. The constrained problem is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:constrained-problem}
\begin{aligned}
\max_\theta\quad
&J_N^{\mathrm{rob}}(\theta)\\
\text{subject to}\quad
&M(\theta)\ge m_0,\\
&Q(\theta)\ge q_0,\\
&\mathbb E_x\!
\left[
\mathrm{KL}
(\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)
\Vert\pi_\mathrm{ref}(\cdot\mid x))
\right]
\le\delta.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

For dual variables \(\lambda_m,\lambda_q\ge0\), define

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lagrangian}
\mathcal J(\theta,\lambda_m,\lambda_q)
=J_N^{\mathrm{rob}}(\theta)
+\lambda_m[M(\theta)-m_0]
+\lambda_q[Q(\theta)-q_0].
\end{equation}

The primal--dual form is
\(\max_\theta
\min_{\lambda_m,\lambda_q\ge0}
\mathcal J(\theta,\lambda_m,\lambda_q)\).
By linearity and the same conditional-centering argument used in
\cref{thm:robust-policy-gradient}, an unbiased response-level
Lagrangian credit is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:constrained-credit}
\widetilde q_i
=q_i^{\mathrm{rob}}
+\frac{\lambda_m}{N}\mu_\omega(x,Y_i)
+\frac{\lambda_q}{N}Q_\eta(x,Y_i).
\end{equation}

The floor constants disappear from the policy score because they do not
depend on sampled responses.  The factor \(1/N\) is necessary:
the constraints concern the expectation of one policy sample, whereas
\(J_N^{\mathrm{rob}}\) concerns a group of \(N\) samples.
When a PPO implementation averages over candidates, multiplying the centered
credit by \(N\) recovers the group-level gradient scale:

\[
N\widetilde q_i
=Nq_i^{\mathrm{rob}}
+\lambda_m\mu_\omega(x,Y_i)
+\lambda_qQ_\eta(x,Y_i).
\]

After an accepted policy update, the projected dual steps are

\begin{align}
\lambda_m
&\leftarrow
\Pi_{[0,\lambda_{m,\max}]}
\left(
\lambda_m+\eta_m[m_0-\widehat M]
\right),\\
\lambda_q
&\leftarrow
\Pi_{[0,\lambda_{q,\max}]}
\left(
\lambda_q+\eta_q[q_0-\widehat Q]
\right).
\label{eq:dual-updates}
\end{align}

The independent Quality RM is a safeguard against model-specific
overoptimization, not a proof that reward hacking is impossible
\citep{gao2023scaling,achiam2017constrained}.

\subsection{PPO realization}
\label{subsec:ppo-realization}

Let \(\pi_\mathrm{old}\) be the behavior policy for the current
rollout group.  For action token \(h\) in response \(i\), define

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:token-ratio}
\rho_{i,h}(\theta)
=\frac{
\pi_\theta(a_{i,h}\mid s_{i,h})}
{\pi_\mathrm{old}(a_{i,h}\mid s_{i,h})}.
\end{equation}

A prompt-only baseline \(b_\psi(x)\) may be subtracted without
bias.  The response advantage assigned to every action token in candidate
\(i\) is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ppo-advantage}
A_i
=N\left[
\widetilde q_i-b_\psi(x)
\right].
\end{equation}

For a minibatch of \(B\) prompts and action-token index set
\(\mathcal T_{b,i}\), the clipped surrogate is

\begin{align}
\widehat{\mathcal S}_{\mathrm{clip}}(\theta)
=\frac{1}{BN}
\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_{b,i}|}
\sum_{h\in\mathcal T_{b,i}}
\min\Bigl(
&\rho_{b,i,h}(\theta)A_{b,i},
\operatorname{clip}(
\rho_{b,i,h}(\theta),
1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{PPO}},
1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{PPO}})
A_{b,i}
\Bigr).
\label{eq:ppo-surrogate}
\end{align}

The implementation maximizes

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regularized-ppo}
\frac{1}{s_k}
\left[
\widehat{\mathcal S}_{\mathrm{clip}}(\theta)
-\beta_k\widehat{\mathrm{KL}}
(\pi_\theta\Vert\pi_\mathrm{ref})
\right],
\end{equation}

where \(s_k>0\) is a lagged RMS scale computed only from previously
accepted batches, and \(\beta_k\) is an adaptive KL coefficient.
The KL estimator is the full-categorical token KL on the current rollout
states.  PPO clipping does not itself enforce a trust region
\citep{schulman2015trust,schulman2017proximal}.  Therefore, before each
tentative optimizer step, the trainable parameters and complete optimizer
state are snapshotted.  If the post-update empirical KL on the same behavior
states exceeds \(\delta_{\mathrm{hard}}\), both snapshots are
restored exactly.  Baseline, dual, RMS, and controller states advance only
after an accepted transition.  This guarantees exact restoration for the
measured batch; it is not a population-level KL theorem.

\subsection{Optimization procedure}
\label{subsec:algorithm}
\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{StableMax-PPO update}
\label{alg:stablemax}
\KwIn{$\mathcal D,\pi_\theta,\pi_{\rm ref},p_\omega,Q_\eta,\varepsilon_{\rm cal},m_0,q_0,N,\delta,\delta_{\rm hard}$}
\KwOut{Updated policy parameters $\theta$.}
Initialize $b_\psi,\lambda_m,\lambda_q,\beta_{\rm KL}$ and lagged scale $s>0$\;
\For{$k=1,\ldots,T_{\rm pol}$}{
  Sample $B$ prompts and $N$ iid responses per prompt from $\pi_{\rm old}$; cache token log-probabilities\;
  Evaluate $p_{\omega,0:4}$, $\mu_\omega$, and $Q_\eta$\;
  Build $\bar F_{b,i}(t)=\min\{1,F_{\omega,b,i}(t)+\varepsilon_{\rm cal}\}$\;
  Compute $q^{\rm rob}_{b,i}$ and $A_{b,i}=N[\tilde q_{b,i}-b_\psi(x_b)]$\;
  Snapshot $(\theta,\mathsf{Opt})$ and take one clipped-PPO step on
  $[\widehat{\mathcal S}_{\rm clip}-\beta_{\rm KL}\widehat{\rm KL}]/s$\;
  \eIf{post-update $\widehat{\rm KL}>\delta_{\rm hard}$}{
    Restore $(\theta,\mathsf{Opt})$; keep controller state unchanged\;
  }{
    Commit the update and advance $b_\psi,\lambda_m,\lambda_q,\beta_{\rm KL},s$\;
  }
}
\end{algorithm}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:stablemax} is the only algorithm in the paper. The
implementation constants and diagnostic definitions are reported in the

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

The finite support of the ordinal model makes the main identities exact. The
following statements isolate what is guaranteed by the objective and what is
only an empirical controller property. Proofs are provided in
Appendix~\ref{app:theory}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-budget maximum]
\label{thm:main-max}
For conditionally independent ratings on $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$,
\(h_N^{\rm nom}=\sum_{t=1}^{4}[1-\prod_jF_{\omega,j}(t-1)]\). Replacing each
CDF by the upper envelope $\bar F_j$ gives the exact worst-case value over
the declared ambiguity set.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Exact marginal policy credit]
\label{thm:main-gradient}
Under (A1)--(A3), the leave-one-out credit in Eq.~\eqref{eq:robust-credit-closed}
is unbiased and satisfies
\(\nabla_\theta J_N^{\rm rob}=\mathbb E[\sum_iq_i^{\rm rob}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(Y_i\mid x)]\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Rollback correctness]
\label{prop:rollback}
If a tentative update is rejected, restoring the parameter and optimizer
snapshots exactly returns the measured rollout-state policy and optimizer to
their pre-update values. This is a batch-level invariant, not a population
KL guarantee.
\end{proposition}

Together, these results separate the mathematical source of the max objective
from the empirical stability mechanism. The calibration envelope supplies a
lower-tail protection, while the KL penalty and rollback bound observed policy
motion. The independent quality floor addresses a complementary failure mode;
none of the three is a claim of universal human-preference superiority.
\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Protocol and questions}
We evaluate four predeclared questions: whether calibrated extreme-value credit improves the held-out robust $\mathbb{E}[\max_{j\leq 32}R_j]$ endpoint; whether any change persists for the nominal ordinal maximum and top-rating probability; whether mean reward, independent quality, and reference-policy KL satisfy their floors; and whether independent generated-output proxies reveal target-reward exploitation. The protocol uses Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct as policy, a frozen Qwen2.5-14B ordinal Moment RM trained from HelpSteer2 repeated helpfulness ratings and preference pairs \citep{wang2024helpsteer2}, and an independently trained Qwen2.5-14B Quality RM. All data partitions and evaluator versions are content-hashed.

Each confirmatory method uses seeds $\{314,2718,1618\}$, 512 test prompts per seed, $N=32$, 300 policy steps, two prompts per step, learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$, one PPO epoch, clip $0.2$, LoRA rank 8, temperature 1, top-$p=1$, maximum prompt length 384 and response length 64. The target KL is 0.02 and the hard rollback level is 0.04. Development seed 42 is excluded from all intervals. Calibration, training, held-out KL, and generated-output evaluation use disjoint prompt partitions.

\subsection{Comparators and analysis}
The primary baselines are Mean-PPO + Q, GRPO + Q, and Scalar Max@32-PPO + Q. Additional comparisons are Entropic-PPO, Nominal Ordinal EV-PPO, Gaussian EV-PPO, Top-4 PPO, and ablations removing the mean or Quality RM floor. An evaluation-only Best-of-32 row samples and ranks 32 base-policy responses and is not a compute-matched training baseline. We report robust and nominal ordinal maxima, probability of at least one rating-four response, candidate mean/variance, independent quality, response length, full-vocabulary held-out KL, constraint residuals, accepted updates, and rollback counts.

The primary aggregate resamples seeds and prompts hierarchically; paired prompt contrasts use two-sided 95\% intervals and one-sided sign-flip tests. All seeds and all predeclared gates are retained. Qwen2.5 and ArmoRM generated-output comparisons are model-based proxies, not human labels. Existing RewardBench and Skywork human-labeled pairs are used only in a static policy-likelihood lockbox.

The artifact verifier passed for seeds 314, 2718, and 1618. Passed gates are the ArmoRM generated-output proxy, held-out KL superiority versus Mean-PPO and GRPO, nominal mean floor, and quality floor. Failed gates are the Qwen generated-output proxy, robust non-inferiority versus Mean-PPO and GRPO, and the primary comparison versus Scalar Max-PPO. Consequently, the preregistered all-gates field is \texttt{scientific\_success=false}. This is not a formatting failure: it records that the method trades some max-oriented endpoint margin for lower KL and quality safeguards. The data support that trade-off, but not direct human preference or absence of reward hacking.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The endpoint is induced by frozen evaluators, and no fresh human labels are
collected for generated outputs. Model proxies can disagree with human
judgments; the 1.5B policy versus 14B evaluator capacity gap may induce
evaluator shift, and three seeds do not establish generalization to other
scales or domains. Full diagnostics, sensitivity analyses, hashes, and
verification records are in Appendix~\ref{app:full-experiments} and the
supplementary artifact.

\paragraph{Reproducibility statement.} The supplementary artifact contains the
exact source, official ICLR style files, bibliography, protocol metadata,
result tables, terminal verification JSON, and build commands. Model weights,
credentials, and private data are excluded.

\paragraph{Ethics statement.} The study uses model-generated text and existing
preference data, involves no human-subject intervention, and releases no
personal data. We disclose proxy limitations and possible reward-model
exploitation.
